Driving from Gurugram to Rishikesh.

The road from Gurugram to Rishikesh, in plain words: how far, which way, when to leave and where to park when you arrive.

Quick facts: Gurugram to Rishikesh by road

  • Gurugram to Rishikesh is about 270 km by road.
  • Plan on about 4.5 to 7 hours on a clear run; traffic and the way out of Gurugram decide the rest.
  • Take the Delhi–Dehradun Expressway to the Dehradun end, then Doiwala and Jolly Grant down to Rishikesh.
  • Leave Gurugram before 5:30 am if you can: the Delhi side of the drive is where the time disappears, and the roads out of the city fill up early.
  • Haridwar to Rishikesh is a 25 km drive up the river.

Getting out of Gurugram

Gurugram is on the far side of Delhi from the road north, so a good part of the first hour goes on crossing the city to Akshardham. The Eastern Peripheral Expressway lets you go around Delhi and meets the Delhi–Dehradun Expressway at Khekra near Baghpat; let live traffic decide which is quicker on the day.

The route

Take the Delhi–Dehradun Expressway to the Dehradun end, then Doiwala and Jolly Grant down to Rishikesh. Navigation apps sometimes send you towards Roorkee and Haridwar instead; let live traffic decide on the day.

Getting out of Gurugram: the options

These are the roads and junctions that matter on the 270 km from Gurugram to Rishikesh.

  • The Dwarka Expressway, about 29 km from Shiv Murti to Kherki Daula, is the signal-free link between Gurugram and the Delhi side. Officials hoped for a 20 to 25 minute run end to end; treat that as a hope, not a promise.
  • The Delhi–Dehradun Expressway starts at Akshardham on the far side of Delhi. Its first 32 km are 12 lanes wide, up to the Khekra junction with the Eastern Peripheral Expressway east of Baghpat; it then continues to Saharanpur and Dehradun.
  • For Haridwar, a 50.7 km spur leaves the main expressway near the 108 km mark and ends at Haridwar's west ring road. One guide puts Delhi to Haridwar at about two hours on it, and Rishikesh roughly 20 km further on NH-58. Check that the spur is open before planning around it.
  • To reach the expressway through Delhi you first cross the border at Sirhaul, or pass IFFCO Chowk, Rajiv Chowk or Hero Honda Chowk. The Delhi–Gurugram Expressway between them is known for jams.
  • The Sirhaul border backs up again and again; one report has an hour passing between Shankar Chowk and the border. Traffic police blame lane-changing for most of the jams on the Delhi–Gurugram Expressway.
  • IFFCO Chowk, Hero Honda Chowk and Rajiv Chowk are among Gurugram's busiest junctions. Rain and waterlogging, notably at Hero Honda Chowk and on the Manesar–Rajiv Chowk stretch, have caused heavy morning jams.

What to avoid

Closures and rules change, so check live traffic and the day's notice before you go to Rishikesh from Gurugram.

  • In the Kanwar Yatra weeks, NH-58 through Muzaffarnagar is shut to every vehicle for about a week and the Delhi–Meerut Expressway to regular traffic. Private cars heading from Delhi towards Haridwar and Dehradun are sent to the Delhi–Dehradun corridor, where peak days see 25 to 30 per cent more traffic. Dates move each year: read the district police notice for your day.
  • Lakshman Jhula was closed to vehicles by the public works department some time ago, after a check on how much two-wheeler traffic the bridge could take; only a limited number of walkers cross. Do not plan to drive or ride over it, and ask your host about the current rule.

Arriving in Rishikesh

  • From Dehradun the direct road to Rishikesh is NH-7 through Jolly Grant, where the airport is, and Doiwala. It is about 45 km, roughly one to one and a half hours.
  • Coming from Haridwar, the Dehradun road runs through Raiwala and Nepali Farm, where a fork leads off to Rishikesh.
