Road trip guide

Driving from Delhi to Rishikesh and Haridwar.

There are now two good ways up from Delhi: the new Delhi–Dehradun Expressway and the old pilgrim highway through Meerut and Haridwar. Here is how to pick one, when to leave, where to eat, and what to do with the car once the Ganga is in sight.

The short answer

Going to Rishikesh? Take the Delhi–Dehradun Expressway to the Dehradun end, then drive down past Doiwala and Jolly Grant airport. On a clear run it is about four hours from east Delhi. Going to Haridwar first? The old highway through Meerut, Muzaffarnagar and Roorkee is still the direct way in, until the new Haridwar spur of the expressway opens. Either way: leave early, carry a working FASTag, and look at live traffic on the morning you drive, because one bad jam can flip the answer.

Route 1: the Delhi–Dehradun Expressway

The whole expressway, from Akshardham in east Delhi to Dehradun, was inaugurated on 14 April 2026. It is a six-lane, access-controlled road through Baghpat, Baraut, Shamli and Saharanpur, with no traffic lights. The best bit is at the end: a long elevated stretch over the Rajaji forest in the Shivalik hills, built so that elephants and deer can walk underneath while you drive over them. Wave if you like. They will not wave back.

  • Speed: the limit for cars is 100 km/h on most of it and lower on the forest stretch near Dehradun. Follow the signboards; speed cameras do the rest.
  • Who can use it: motorcycles, autos and tractors are not allowed on at least the Delhi end, and riders are sent to the old highway. Riding up on a bike? Check the current rules before you plan around it.
  • Tolls: five toll plazas. At opening, a car paid roughly ₹670–675 one way, and around ₹1,000 for a return within 24 hours. Rates get revised, so treat these as a guide and check before you go.
  • Fuel and food: petrol pumps, food courts and toilets along the expressway were still being finished when it opened. Leave Delhi with a full tank, water and snacks, and do not count on a stop that may not be open yet.
  • The last hour to Rishikesh: from the Dehradun end, follow signs for Rishikesh via Doiwala and Jolly Grant airport. Navigation apps sometimes send you off earlier towards Roorkee and Haridwar; let live traffic decide on the day.

Starting from Gurugram or south Delhi? Just getting across the city to Akshardham can take as long as the first 100 km of expressway. One more reason to leave before 6 am.

Route 2: the old pilgrim highway via Meerut and Haridwar

This is the road generations of families took to Haridwar: the Delhi–Meerut Expressway out of the city, then the old Delhi–Haridwar highway (numbered NH-334 now, still called NH-58 by everyone) through Muzaffarnagar and Roorkee, and finally the 25 km run up the river from Haridwar to Rishikesh. It goes through more towns, so expect slow patches around Roorkee, the Haridwar approach and Raiwala, but it is the direct route to Haridwar and it has the famous food stops.

The Haridwar spur: a new six-lane link is being built from the expressway near Saharanpur to Haridwar's ring road at Bahadrabad, skipping Roorkee. Work was in its final stages at our last check and it is meant to be ready before Kumbh 2027, but we will believe it when our own tyres touch it. Check whether it has opened before you plan around it.

Where to break the journey and eat

Khatauli, old highway

Cheetal Grand at Khatauli, roughly halfway to Haridwar, has fed Haridwar-bound families for decades: pakoras, cutlets, dosas, clean washrooms and a proper stretch of the legs. There are plenty of dhabas around Muzaffarnagar and Roorkee too.

On the expressway

Food courts and fuel stops are part of the plan along the expressway, but some were not open yet when it launched. Pack breakfast, or wait for Dehradun, which has no shortage of places to eat.

Haridwar

If you come in through Haridwar, park and walk to the lanes around Har Ki Pauri for puri-sabzi, kachori and lassi. Do not try to drive close to the ghats; the lanes will win.

Save room for Rishikesh

The town is vegetarian and alcohol-free, and the cafes are half the fun. See our restaurants and cafes guide for where to eat on arrival.

Tolls and FASTag

  • Make sure your FASTag is active and topped up before you leave. Toll plazas have been moving to FASTag and UPI only, and drivers without a valid FASTag pay more than the normal fee.
  • Have the FASTag annual pass for your private car? Check in your FASTag or Rajmargyatra app that it covers the plazas on your route and how many trips it will use.
  • Toll rates are revised from time to time. The figures on this page are a guide, not a quote.

When to leave Delhi

  • Before 6 am is the magic hour, on weekdays and weekends alike. You clear Delhi before it wakes up and reach Rishikesh in time for a late breakfast by the river.
  • Avoid Friday evening. Half of Delhi has the same idea as you, and the queue at the Rishikesh end on a long weekend can be longer than the drive.
  • Drive back early. Leave Rishikesh after an early breakfast on Sunday, or better, on Monday morning. Sunday afternoon is when everyone heads home at once.
  • Winter fog: in December and January the plains can be thick with morning fog. Leave a little later, after it lifts, rather than crawl through it in the dark.
  • Monsoon: July to September brings heavy showers and waterlogging. The drive is usually fine, but check road updates if you are going further up into the hills.

Festival rush: Kanwar Yatra and Kumbh 2027

Kanwar Yatra falls in the month of Sawan, roughly July to August. Lakhs of Kanwariyas walk and ride to Haridwar to collect Ganga water, and the Delhi–Meerut–Haridwar highway gets heavy-vehicle bans, diversions and, on the peak days, closures for all vehicles. In 2026 the expressway stayed open as an alternate road while Kanwar groups were kept off it, but the plan changes every year. If you can, do not drive in the last week of the yatra. If you must, take the expressway, skip Haridwar, and keep an eye on the traffic advisories.

Kumbh 2027 brings Haridwar its biggest crowds in years. On the main bathing days, roads near the ghats close to vehicles and traffic is diverted around the city. Rishikesh, about 25 km upriver, makes a calmer base for the Kumbh. See our Haridwar Kumbh 2027 guide for the reported dates and how to plan around them. Big festivals, Diwali week and New Year fill the roads too, so book your stay early and leave even earlier.

Parking in Rishikesh: the lanes have opinions

Rishikesh was built for pilgrims on foot, not for SUVs. The lanes in Tapovan, around Laxman Jhula and in Swarg Ashram are narrow, steep in places, and shared with scooters, cows and very determined chai sellers. The famous suspension footbridges are walking bridges, not car bridges.

  • Ask your stay about parking before you book. Many guest houses in Tapovan have none at all.
  • Use a paid parking lot on the main road and walk the last stretch, or hop on an e-rickshaw.
  • On weekend evenings the Tapovan and Laxman Jhula roads can lock up completely. Park early and explore on foot.
  • Going rafting? Most rafting operators pick you up from town, so the car can stay parked.

Arrived! Now what?

  1. Check in, stretch, and walk down to the Ganga. Four hours of highway melts quickly at the water's edge.
  2. Catch the evening Ganga Aarti at Triveni Ghat or Parmarth Niketan. In Haridwar, it is the big aarti at Har Ki Pauri.
  3. Have an early dinner and an early night. Tomorrow is for rafting, yoga, a waterfall walk or a temple sunrise; see things to do in Rishikesh.

Delhi to Rishikesh by road: questions travellers ask

How long does it take to drive from Delhi to Rishikesh?

On the Delhi–Dehradun Expressway it is about 2.5 to 3 hours from Akshardham to Dehradun, then roughly an hour more to Rishikesh via Doiwala and Jolly Grant, so around 4 hours on a clear run. Add time for getting across Delhi and for Dehradun traffic. The old route via Meerut, Muzaffarnagar, Roorkee and Haridwar usually takes 6 to 7 hours.

Is the Delhi–Dehradun Expressway fully open?

Yes. The whole six-lane expressway from Akshardham in Delhi to Dehradun was inaugurated on 14 April 2026. Some fuel stations, food courts and rest areas were still being finished when it opened, so check what is running before you rely on them.

How much is the toll from Delhi to Dehradun on the expressway?

At opening, a car paid roughly ₹670 to ₹675 one way across five toll plazas, with a return within 24 hours costing around ₹1,000. Toll rates are revised from time to time, so check the current rate in your FASTag or Rajmargyatra app. Use a working FASTag: drivers without one pay more.

Which is the best route from Delhi to Haridwar?

For Haridwar, the old highway via Meerut, Muzaffarnagar and Roorkee is still the direct route. A new six-lane spur from the expressway near Saharanpur to Haridwar's ring road at Bahadrabad, bypassing Roorkee, is being built and is meant to be ready before Kumbh 2027. Check whether it has opened before you go, and compare live traffic on the morning you drive.

When should I avoid driving from Delhi to Rishikesh or Haridwar?

Avoid the Kanwar Yatra fortnight in the month of Sawan (roughly July to August), when the Delhi–Meerut–Haridwar highway sees closures and diversions, and the main bathing days of Kumbh 2027 in Haridwar. On ordinary weekends, leave Delhi before 6 am, skip Friday evening, and drive back early on Sunday or on Monday morning.

Where can I park in Rishikesh?

Tapovan, Laxman Jhula and Swarg Ashram have narrow lanes and limited parking, and the river footbridges are not for cars. Ask your stay about parking before you book, or use a paid lot on the main road and walk.
